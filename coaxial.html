Coaxial swerve, is the far more straightforward way to do a drivetrain. For movement, it is simply powered by 1 motor per pod, with a single servo to power azimuth rotation. due to using a coaxial shaft, it suffers from the effects of coaxial rotation, otherwise known as the twin "coax effect" the coax effect comes in two parts. the first is due to imperfect bearings, and thus, rotation of the central shaft, powering the wheel, ends up actually moving the "pod"/the section that holds the axle of the wheel with it, changing its angle. This unwanted movement fights the attempts of the servo that is meant to control rotation, and can mess with your tuning and steering. the second is a consequence of the gear, geared to the central shaft, moving around it as the pod is moved. As the pod moves, the gear attatched to the shaft geared via a bevel to the wheel rotates respective to a stationary observer, as well as the pod itself. You can calculate it as exactly the gear ratio between the gear of the central shaft and the outer gear. this means that when the pod is rotated, the physical robot is moved as well, forcing the servos to actually do some "driving" action. This puts extra strain on the servos. More about these will be continued in HARDWARE.
