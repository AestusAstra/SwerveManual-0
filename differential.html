DIFFERENTIAL

INTRODUCTION

a differential is fundamentally, a gear system that enables two different rpms in as input, that enable two different outputs in two different axis, with the unpowered output "idling" and spinning in place. They are often used in cars, or in the case of FTC,

differential swerve, instead of having an individual azimuth rotation actuator via servo, and movement powered via motor, it utilizes a differential gear system in order to acheive azimuth rotation as well as wheel rotation. moving the larger two parallel bevel gears in opposite directions at the same speed enables directional movement via the rotation of the small bevel gear connected to a shaft, while mvoing them in the same speed enables rapid azimuth rotation.

Pros:
no servos required
can handle a heavier robot as azimuth rotation has more power
faster azimuth rotation
less current draw
significantly better handling (no swerve wobble )
cons:
only has 2 wheels when 4 motors used, meaning possibly less traction than coax. Crab Diffy also exists, where the rotation of two pods are linked together on each side, allowing 4 wheels. This however, makes changing the heading of the robot, or turning a lot harder.
often worse spacing, and often more difficult to package
can be more complex, both mechanically and programming wise. No well tested or documented auto solutions.

Bevel diffy

(commonly known as gluten-free diffy, after the team that introduced diffy to FTC, and the GOAT)

this type of diffy typically uses bevel gears as its core differential. this diffy has the differential around the center wheel, with the two larger bevel gears encircling it, and the smaller bevel being sandwiched between then, and connected to the center axle of the wheel.

this type of diffy tends to also forgo using actual bearings and have both the supports for the axle and the larger bevel gears be supported by a large disc riding V-Groove bearings instead of large X-Contact bearings. V-Groove bearings, while cheap, come with many issues, as creating the disc profile to ride on them requires you carefully undersize them, otherwise they will get stuck. however, undersizing them too much comes with backlash, and fragility. They also are bulky and much larger than using x-contact bearings, and not as smooth. Overall, V-Grooves are not reccomended. This type of diffy requires 3d printed bevel gears that can be difficult to get right, and are often larger and more difficult to package comapred to spur diffies.

Spur gear diffy

(commonly known as Tayor-diffy, after the man, the myth, the legend, the late Anthony Taylor)

This type of diffy came about later than bevel diffy. It utilized spur gears to create a differential effect. It has an advantage of being able to be done entirely with steel spur COTS (commercial, off the shelf) gears from common manufacterers like GoBILDA. If one wants to get custom gears to push the archetype further though, SLM (metal 3d printing) you have that option as well. This swerve tends to be much easier to "get right the first time", and is highly robust. Spur diffies as of now, have the edge, and are thus reccomended if you actually intend to use them during competition
