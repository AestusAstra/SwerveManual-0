THE COAX EFFECT

the coax effect is a known issue that comes to coaxial swerves, and it comes in two parts. the first is due to imperfect bearings, causing rotation of the central shaft, which powering the wheel, to end up actually moving the "pod" with it, changing its angle. This unwanted movement fights the attempts of the servo that is meant to control rotation, and can mess with your tuning and steering. the second is a consequence of the gear, geared to the central shaft, moving around it as the pod is moved. As the pod moves, the gear attatched to the shaft geared via a bevel to the wheel rotates respective to a stationary observer, as well as the pod itself. You can calculate it as exactly the gear ratio between the gear of the central shaft and the outer gear. this means that when the pod is rotated, the physical robot is moved as well, forcing the servos to actually do some "driving" action. This puts extra strain on the servos.

COMMON ISSUES

Encoders:

Improper configuration can cause the software to confuse which encoder corresponds to which pod, causing issues.

Bad wiring can also mess with encoder readings, as frayed, torn, or otherwise chipped wires will disrupt readings. Bad connections on the Axon 4th wire encoder are very common and often overlooked. Always check the quality of the crimp on connectors.

Dysfunctional encoders can give inaccurate, inconsistent, or unstable readings. Causes of this include simple breaking of the motor, hall effect sensor, any internal power transfers, and firmware crashes. Firmware crashes are especially common on the sensorange encoders.

Encoders must track the physical rotation of the pod, and so a disruption of the transmission that transfers the rotation of the pod to the encoder, via losses in motion like backlash can cause error to build up.

Improper gearing will also do the same, as an absolute encoder can only track it accurately if the pod is 1:1 with the encoder.

Over/under gearing:

Overgearing can result in high power draw, blown fuses, and slow driving due to the wheels not having enough torque for the robot. Always check using a drivetrain simulator like ILITE that your gearing is feasible for the given mass of the robot.

Robot Weight:

A sufficiently heavy robot will lead to further issues, as the axon mini will begin to struggle to power the azimuth rotation for the wheels. This is why it is reccomended to keep your robot under 13kg if you are running coaxial swerve. fragility: poorly designed parts, especially 3d printed ones can bend, flex and break. Improper tension: lack of tension can cause belts to skip resulting in lost motion. Too much tension can lead to a loss in efficency and parts "locking up".
