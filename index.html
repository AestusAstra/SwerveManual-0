INTRODUCTION

Have you heard the story of Darth Saket the wise? Its not a story that the solvers would tell you.

SwerveDrives are one of the most complex and difficult mechanisms in FTC, in an area where improved performance is often underapreciated. They can be difficult to program and design, with many unique frustrations. given the existence of mecanums, a much simpler option with consistent viability no matter the game, Swerve is hardly ever worth it.

Swerve is a pathway to many abilities some consider to be ... unnatural...

On the other hand, when they do work, Swervedrives can be absolutely broken. Kneebreaking maneuvers, increased traction, insane speed, and great defensive properties all come with the package of a working swervedrive.

Do you think you have what it takes?

That said, SwerveDrives are far from perfect. They come with a myriad of mechanical and software issues not encountered elsewhere and there is a significant amount of obscure, esoteric knowledge involved. Learning all of it via trial and error wears down ones resolve, and eats into the season.

So you want to build a SwerveDrive?

If you're still going to do this, you better do it right. Swerve Manual 0 has all of the required knowledge in one place.
First, make sure that you have at least 4-5 of following:
enough budget
dedication
experience
at least 1 good CADer
at least 1 good programmer
and a solid plan and understanding of swervedrives

BEFORE WE BEGIN

Make sure that swerve is even viable in the current season. For a comeptition that is movement heavy, such as centerstage or DECODE, a swervedrive might be the deciding factor in a match. However, in seasons where the ideal robot eliminates movement entirely, such as Into The Deep, swervedrives are almsot useless. while the aura is undeniable, its not nearly as worth it if the game isnt movement based.


While not mandatory, here are some useful tools you should get beforehand:

Hardware: - Onshape, Fusion360, Solidworks all work. However, Onshape is reccomended with its wide availability of featurescripts and plugins as well as its parts library. simbas Cad lib ftc catalog part lighten beltgen a GoBILDA SPI (compatible with photonCore, and strengthens servos for azimuth if you are using coaxial swerve)

Software: Android studio, preferably linked to a github repository some sort of swerve autonomous library, such as ivy, wakeup, or pedro FTC dashboard, or panels (dashboard preferred) sloth some form of caching for motors, servos, and CRServos, either manually, from a wrapper, or an external library like dairy PhotonCore (or at least some basic multithreading) Usb mini cable (for photoncore) wire everything swerve related to the rev control hub. Axon mk2 minis, that have never been programmed (for swerve mode)

Swerve Examples:

Some swerves I created, and I think will be useful for reference for anyone who wants to get a better understanding

Stellar Swerve

Swerves I created specifically for open sourcing. Made to be easy to adopt and adapt. Both a Coaxial and differential are included. However, both are imperfect, and can be improved significantly. I reccomend you use these merely as examples of a decent, functional swerve, and do not use the CAD files directly unless the situation is DIRE. Using them without understanding the principles behind them can lead to issues building, maintaining, tuning, and programming them.

Barracuda

A swerve I created in 2 days specifically for the CHAIN REACTION CAD competition. It pushes what is possible with manufacturing , featuring SLM gears, allowing for the integration of a center bore mounted magnet that allows the encoder to read the position of the swerve pod directly, without any risk of interference from backlash. I do think, however, it could be pushed further.
